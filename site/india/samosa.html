Samosa — Authentic & Heartfelt

A warm, aromatic samosa recipe inspired by the classic kitchens of India. Crisp pastry, fragrant filling — perfect for chai-time.
Cuisine: Indian
Overview

This samosa focuses on a gently-spiced potato and green-pea filling — scented with warm spices, fennel and a hint of lime. It’s crisp and golden, ideal with mint and tamarind chutney.

Prep: 30 mins
Cook: 25 mins
Rest: 30 mins (dough)
Serves: 4 (≈12 small samosas)

Ingredients

For the Dough

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour (maida)
  • 3 tbsp ghee (or oil) + extra for sealing
  • ½ tsp salt
  • Warm water, as needed (approx. ⅔ cup)

For the Filling

  • 3 medium potatoes, boiled and mashed
  • ½ cup green peas (fresh/frozen)
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 1 tsp fennel seeds (saunf) — lightly crushed
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped (optional)
  • 1–2 green chillies, finely chopped (adjust to taste)
  • 1 tsp grated ginger
  • ½ tsp turmeric powder
  • 1 tsp coriander powder
  • ½ tsp garam masala
  • Salt to taste
  • Juice of ½ lemon
  • Small handful fresh coriander (cilantro), chopped
Tip: Use waxy potatoes for creamier texture; mash lightly keeping a few small pieces for bite.

Instructions — Step by Step

1. Prepare the dough.

In a bowl, combine the flour and salt. Rub in the ghee (or oil) until the mixture looks like coarse breadcrumbs. Add warm water little by little and gently knead into a soft, firm dough. Cover with a damp cloth and rest for 30 minutes.

2. Cook the filling.

Heat oil in a pan. Add cumin and crushed fennel seeds; let them sizzle. Add chopped onion (if using), ginger and green chillies; sauté until soft. Add turmeric, coriander powder and peas — cook 2–3 minutes. Stir in the mashed potatoes, salt and garam masala. Cook on low for 3–4 minutes, add lemon juice and coriander leaves. Taste and adjust seasoning. Let the filling cool.

3. Shape the samosas.

Divide dough into 10–12 equal balls. Roll each ball into an oval (about 5–6 inches). Cut oval in half. Take one semi-circle, form a cone by folding, seal the edge with a little water, fill with 1–2 tbsp of filling, fold the top and seal firmly using a bit of dough-water mix.

4. Frying.

Heat oil in a deep pan on medium. Test with a small piece of dough; it should sizzle and rise slowly. Fry samosas in batches on medium heat until golden brown, turning to cook evenly (about 4–6 minutes per batch). Drain on paper towels.

5. Serve and enjoy.

Let samosas rest 2–3 minutes; serve warm with mint chutney and sweet tamarind chutney, and a steaming cup of chai. For an Awadhi touch, garnish with a sprinkle of chaat masala or finely chopped coriander.

Storage: Keep in an airtight container at room temperature for 1 day. Reheat in an oven/air-fryer to restore crispiness.

Pro Tips from Indian kitchens

  • Lightly crush fennel (saunf) — it gives a subtle aroma that distinguishes samosas.
  • Use ghee for dough if you want a richer flavor; mixing ghee + oil gives a crisp shell.
  • Seal samosas with a good pinch of dough and a gentle press to avoid bursting while frying.
  • Don’t overfill — small, well-sealed samosas fry more evenly.
